# TeacherOS Day 2 Behavioral Interview Guide

## Purpose

Learn how an English teacher handled the last real planning, marking, adapting, evidence-analysis, or reporting task. The interview is not a product demo and not a request for feature ideas.

Target duration: 25-30 minutes. Interview at least five eligible teachers who teach two or more recurring English lessons weekly.

## Before the call

1. Assign the participant an anonymous ID such as `P01`. Do not put their name or contact details in Git.
2. Ask them to bring one recent artifact they are allowed to show: an anonymized lesson plan, worksheet, marking notes, class-level error tally, or report. It must contain no student names or identifying details.
3. Explain that they may skip any question and may refuse recording.
4. Store any recording or raw artifact only under `research_private/day02/`.
5. Open the workbook's **Interviews** sheet and use the matching `INT-##` row.

## Opening and consent: 2 minutes

Say:

> Thank you. I am studying how English teachers actually prepare, adapt, review, and report their work. I am not testing you, and I am not trying to sell you anything today. Please do not share student names or private student information. May I take de-identified notes? If you brought an artifact, may I record only an anonymous artifact ID and observations about your workflow?

Do not continue without permission to take de-identified notes.

## Eligibility and context: 3 minutes

Ask:

1. What kinds of English classes do you teach?
2. How many lessons do you teach in a normal week?
3. How many are recurring classes where you see the same learners again?
4. Is Telegram part of your daily teaching or coordination workflow? How?

Exclude the interview from the Day 2 minimum if the teacher has fewer than two recurring lessons weekly. Keep the notes only as clearly labeled non-target context.

## Last real task timeline: 10 minutes

Ask for one recent, specific event:

1. Think about the last lesson you planned, marked, adapted, or reported. When was it?
2. What triggered the task?
3. What did you do first?
4. What did you open or look for next?
5. Walk me through every step until the work was usable or submitted.
6. Where did you copy, retype, search, wait, or switch tools?
7. Approximately how many minutes did the complete task take?
8. What part took the most time?
9. What did you leave unfinished or do less carefully because of time?
10. What happened in the class or reporting process afterward?

Use neutral probes: “What happened next?”, “Can you show me?”, “How do you know?”, and “What did you do the last time?”

Do not ask, “Would an AI class-memory feature help?” or “Do you like this idea?”

## Artifact walkthrough: 7 minutes

Ask the teacher to show the anonymized artifact without sending it if they prefer.

1. What is this artifact and why did you create it?
2. Which part came from an existing source?
3. Which part required judgment or adaptation?
4. What did you check before using or sharing it?
5. What would make this artifact unsafe, embarrassing, or unusable?
6. What changed after you saw student performance or class outcomes?
7. What information would you refuse to upload to an AI service?

Record only an anonymous artifact ID such as `ART-P01-01`. In the workbook, note the witnessed steps and consequence, not a long quotation.

## Outcome, trust, and commercial evidence: 5 minutes

Ask:

1. What would a successful result have looked like?
2. How did you decide the work was good enough?
3. What is the consequence when this task is rushed or wrong?
4. Have you paid for a tool, template, course, or service to improve this task? What caused that decision?
5. If a trustworthy tool reliably saved time on this exact task, how would you decide whether to pay? Do not ask for a price first.
6. Would you ever upload anonymized student evidence? Under what conditions? What would make you refuse?

Translate the answers into the workbook's 1-5 fields only after the interview, using the scoring anchors below.

## Pilot invitation: 2 minutes

Say:

> I may run a small later pilot where teachers complete a real planning-to-outcome loop in Telegram. It will require honest feedback and may include rough edges. May I contact you about it?

Record `interested`, `confirmed`, or `declined`. “Interested” is not “confirmed.”

## Scoring anchors

Use whole numbers from 1 to 5:

- Frequency: 1 = less than monthly; 2 = monthly; 3 = weekly; 4 = several times weekly; 5 = almost every lesson.
- Time loss: 1 = under 5 minutes; 2 = 5-15; 3 = 16-30; 4 = 31-60; 5 = over 60 minutes.
- Consequence: 1 = cosmetic inconvenience; 2 = mild friction; 3 = lesson/report quality suffers; 4 = meaningful learner/teacher consequence; 5 = serious trust, privacy, financial, or professional harm.
- Upload willingness: 1 = refuses even anonymized evidence; 3 = maybe with clear controls; 5 = willing with consent, anonymization, deletion, and provider clarity.
- Pay willingness: 1 = no evidence and would not consider; 3 = considers if value is demonstrated; 5 = has paid for this job or states a concrete purchase/approval condition.

## Immediate post-interview synthesis

Within 15 minutes:

1. Complete the de-identified workbook row.
2. Assign one primary problem code only. Add secondary observations to notes without inflating independent counts.
3. Confirm minutes, artifact count, consequence, success proof, and trust concern are present.
4. Separate what the teacher did from what they suggested.
5. Mark uncertainties explicitly.

Do not select the top problems until all five minimum interviews are complete.
